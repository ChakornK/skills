# Brainstorming and Idea Generation

Use this reference when the user asks for an idea, suggestion, concept, option, approach, or
recommendation, including prompt, skill, or process ideas. Do not use it to force an ideation funnel
onto execution of a specific creative brief.

## Generate

Frame the goal and constraints. Explore distinct mechanisms, audiences, formats, scales, and
assumptions. Make successive novelty passes over underrepresented dimensions—such as actor, timing,
incentive, information, context, and failure response. Name distinct candidates and stop when further
angles produce repetitions rather than new directions.

## Narrow

Use a sequential champion ladder. Label candidates C1 through CN in generation order; C1 is the initial
champion, and each next unused candidate challenges it once. For every match, identify the challenger’s
defining advantage and the specific problem it addresses. Compare both candidates on that same problem,
stating a concrete benefit, cost, or failure mode for each. Either may win; advance the challenger when
its advantage matters more to the goal. Give the loser’s reason and use one consistent tie-breaker.

Show the full roster and a compact ledger with exactly N−1 rows. Each row records incumbent, challenger,
the target problem, concrete comparison, winner, loser and reason, and resulting champion. Audit that
match one is C1 vs C2, later incumbents equal prior winners, challengers C2 through CN appear once in
order, and one survivor remains. Fix any mismatch before recommending.

## Refine and validate

Treat the finalist as provisional. State success/failure criteria and operationalize it as a prompt,
procedure, or rule. For prompt ideas, show separate blocks for exact prompt, sample input, and generated
output. Apply the prompt text to the sample; do not substitute a paraphrase of an expected answer. If
that application was not actually performed, label it simulated. For procedures or rules, carry out the
steps and record outcomes.

Identify a concrete failure, revise to address it, and apply the revision to a materially different
sample against the same criteria. If it fails, revise and retest; also test a relevant stress case.
Finish with a distinct fresh confirmation using the final version and captured output. Assess each
result before recommending. Tests support claims only about their samples, not external facts or
universal reliability. State remaining validation limits and keep the final recommendation last.
